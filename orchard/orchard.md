## 2. Rootstock notes

### EMLA 7

*Source: `Orchard!A16:G27`*

EMLA 7: EMLA 7 is a semi-dwarf apple rootstock that produces a medium-sized tree, usually much smaller and easier to manage than a full standard tree. It is a good fit for cider apple rows where you want earlier production, easier pruning, easier picking, and better control over canopy size. The tradeoff is that EMLA 7 can produce root suckers and may not anchor as strongly as larger rootstocks, so young trees often benefit from staking or support, especially in windy areas. For your Dabinett, Yarlington Mill, Kingston Black, and Ribston Pippin rows, EMLA 7 makes sense because it keeps the main cider varieties more manageable and productive in a smaller space.

### EMLA 111

*Source: `Orchard!H16:N27`*

EMLA 111 is a larger, tougher semi-standard rootstock that produces a much bigger tree than EMLA 7. It is better anchored, more drought-tolerant, and generally more forgiving in tougher orchard conditions, which makes it useful where long-term durability matters. The downside is that it takes more space, can be slower to come into heavy production, and may require more pruning to keep it from outgrowing the rest of the orchard. For your Yellow Newton row, EMLA 111 is a reasonable choice because it gives that heritage apple a stronger, more resilient root system, but you will want to train it early so it does not become much larger than the EMLA 7 rows.
