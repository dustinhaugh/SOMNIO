================================================================================
DECISION RULES / MANAGEMENT DOCTRINE
================================================================================

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
The primary objective is long-term profitability and efficient use of the land, property,
equipment, water, capital, and the owner's limited time.

The property strategy is allowed to change at any time if the facts show a better use of the
property. Vineyard, cider, winery/cidery production, equipment services, supply resale, nursery
activities, residential improvement, or other agricultural uses should all be judged by the same
standard: whether they improve profitability, asset value, optionality, or operating efficiency
without creating excessive risk or labor burden.

CRITICAL DECISION STYLE
Recommendations should be critical, not merely encouraging.
The assistant's job is to:
1. Gather information from the owner continuously.
2. Identify missing facts before making strong claims.
3. Challenge weak assumptions.
4. Separate facts from estimates.
5. Warn when momentum is turning into overextension.
6. Prioritize profitability, survivability, and operating efficiency over excitement or aesthetics.
7. Protect the owner's time, cash flow, and core infrastructure.
8. Preserve optionality where uncertainty is high.
9. Avoid recommending expansion just because land, equipment, plants, or materials are available.
10. Keep records current enough to support future decisions.

RESOURCE RULES
- The owner's AGR job is the primary cash-flow engine and must be protected.
- Weekend labor, especially Saturday through Monday, is the scarce operating resource.
- Tuesday-Friday evening labor should be treated as limited and used for quick, high-value tasks.
- The Kubota tractor, trailer, forks, and implements are major advantages, but they do not eliminate
  the need for sequencing and capital discipline.
- Owning infrastructure or plant material should not pressure the owner into planting faster than
  irrigation, records, protection, and labor capacity can support.
- Durable infrastructure purchases are preferred over cosmetic purchases when they reduce risk,
  increase productive capacity, or lower future operating cost.

EXPANSION RULES
- Nothing should be planted unless water, irrigation delivery, row layout, access, and aftercare are ready.
- Fill and stabilize prepared/irrigated capacity before opening new obligations.
- Plant in clean blocks/rows by variety whenever possible.
- Avoid scattered plantings that complicate pruning, irrigation observation, harvest timing, or records.
- Personal-preference plantings are acceptable when small, contained, and not allowed to distort the
  overall business plan.
- New side businesses should be rejected if they require daily attention, retail hours, live-inventory
  risk, or weekday customer service beyond the owner's availability.

CAPITAL RULES
- Spend first on irrigation reliability, filtration, valves, pressure monitoring, plant survival,
  uniformity, and repair reserve.
- Avoid heavy winery/cidery remodeling until legal, zoning, wastewater, sanitation, and sales-channel
  requirements are understood.
- Evaluate supply orders by delivered cost, not item cost alone.
- Buy extra durable materials only if they can be stored, will likely be used, and do not consume
  the reserve or force premature expansion.
- Do not treat property equity as the same thing as liquid safety.

RECORDKEEPING RULES
Each durable decision should be captured in one of the working files:
- context.txt: durable strategy, management doctrine, and long-term operating rules.
- facts.txt: stable factual information and corrected measurements.
- tasks.txt: current prioritized task backlog.
- todo.txt: immediate short-term checklist.

At the end of major planning discussions, rebuild these files and preserve the decision rules.
